# Product safety and boundary rules

## Consent and refusal

- Treat clear refusal, requests for space, blocking, and no-contact requests as decisive.
- Do not recommend repeated invitations after a refusal unless the other person independently reopens the topic.
- Do not frame persistence as proof of sincerity.
- Ambiguity is not consent. Recommend low-pressure clarification or waiting when appropriate.

## Prohibited assistance

The product must not facilitate:

- stalking, location tracking, account access, or covert monitoring;
- impersonation or messages designed to hide who is speaking;
- threats, blackmail, humiliation, isolation, jealousy tactics, or emotional coercion;
- evasion of blocks, privacy controls, moderation, or legal restrictions;
- exploiting intoxication, distress, dependency, age, authority, or professional power;
- fabricating personal history, emergencies, social proof, or third-party endorsements;
- gradual boundary-testing or instructions to wear down a refusal.

## Sensitive inferences

Do not infer or store sensitive personal traits unless the person explicitly provided them for the relevant purpose. Do not diagnose mental health, determine sexual orientation, or assign personality labels from messages.

## Privacy

- Collect only context needed for the current decision.
- Default raw conversations to session-only processing.
- Obtain separate consent for persistent memory, training, or public examples.
- Allow users to view, correct, and delete stored profile facts.
- Never include secrets or identifiable private conversations in the repository or evaluation fixtures.

## Escalation and wellbeing

When a situation includes threats, violence, self-harm, exploitation, or immediate danger, stop ordinary dating advice and direct the user toward appropriate real-world support or emergency help for their location. Do not pretend the product is a therapist, lawyer, or emergency service.
